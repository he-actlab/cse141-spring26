Introduction to Computer Architecture

MWF 10:00–10:50am CSE 1202 UC San Diego
UC San Diego

This course covers the fundamentals of modern computer architecture, from instruction-set design and pipelining to the memory hierarchy and I/O. We study how hardware and software interact and learn to reason about performance, correctness, and design tradeoffs across the stack.

By the end of the quarter, you will understand how real processors are built — including the microarchitectural techniques that power the CPUs and accelerators driving today's systems.

Prerequisites: CSE 110 or equivalent. Familiarity with C/C++ and basic digital logic is helpful but not required.

Discussion sections meet weekly. Attendance is strongly encouraged — worked problems and exam review material are covered in section and are not repeated in lecture.

Lecture slides and readings are posted to this page the evening before each class. Assignments are submitted through Gradescope. Course announcements are on Piazza.

Textbook: Computer Organization and Design: ARM Edition, Patterson & Hennessy. Readings are listed in the schedule. A limited number of copies are on 2-hour reserve at Geisel Library.

Course policies

Attendance. Lectures are not formally recorded. You are responsible for all material covered in class. Collaboration on understanding lecture content is encouraged.

Homework. Four problem-set homeworks, submitted via Gradescope (PDF only, show all work). You may discuss problems with classmates but must write up solutions independently. Late work accepted up to 48 hours with a 20% penalty; no credit after 48 hours.

Exams. Two in-class midterms and one final. All exams are closed-book; one double-sided 8.5×11" note sheet is allowed per midterm, two for the final. If you miss a midterm due to a documented emergency, the weight will be redistributed to the final.

Academic integrity. All submitted work must be your own. Sharing solutions, using AI-generated answers, or copying from any source constitutes a violation and will be reported to the Academic Integrity Office.

Regrades. Submit regrade requests through Gradescope within one week of grades being released. Describe specifically which rubric item you believe was applied incorrectly.
