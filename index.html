Introduction to Computer Architecture

MWF 10:00–10:50am CSE 1202 UC San Diego
UC San Diego

This course covers the basics of modern processor design and operation. Topics include instruction set architectures, computer system performance, machine organization, pipelining, branch prediction, memory-hierarchy design, and an introduction to multiprocessor considerations.

This course is designed to run alongside CSE 141L. We expect that you are enrolled in both.

Prerequisites: CSE 110 or equivalent. Familiarity with C/C++ and basic digital logic is helpful.

Lecture slides and readings are posted to the Schedule tab before each class. Assignments submitted through Gradescope (via Canvas). Q&A on Piazza. Office hours on Canvas.

Textbook: Computer Organization and Design MIPS Edition, 5th Ed. (Patterson & Hennessy). ISBN-13: 978-0124077263. Required.

Other recommended reading:

  • Hennessy & Patterson, Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach — more advanced, broader coverage.
  • Synthesis Lectures on Computer Architecture — outstanding recent books on processor microarchitecture, memory, and more; free on the UCSD network.

The details in this syllabus may change as the course progresses. Updates will be announced on Piazza.
Participation (Mini-Quizzes) — 5%

During lectures we will have interactive Q&A segments (polls). In-lecture polls are not graded for correctness or attendance. At the end of each week, all poll questions are collected into a mini-quiz on Canvas, due by midnight the following Tuesday. Graded for completion, not correctness.

Late policy: No extensions. Two bonus quizzes (before midterm, before final) can make up a missed week; they will not count for extra credit.

Homework — 20%

Longer-form assignments to test individual understanding. Discuss in groups, but write up solutions alone. Submit via Gradescope (PDF). Regrade requests via Gradescope within one week of grades being returned.

Generally released Thursday, due the following Thursday at midnight. Expect ~7–8 assignments, with exceptions around exams and holidays.

Late policy: Accepted any time before the final exam at 50% of earned points. Not eligible for regrades. Must clearly show your own work.

Midterm — 30%

One in-class midterm on Friday, May 9. Closed-book; one double-sided 8.5×11" note sheet. 50 minutes.

Final Exam — 45%

Cumulative. Registrar-assigned: Saturday, June 14, 11:30am–2:29pm, CSE 1202. Two double-sided note sheets. If Saturday is an absolute conflict, contact the instructor well in advance.

Academic integrity

Cheating will be taken seriously. Not cheating: discussing homework in groups (writeups done separately). Cheating: using someone's completed work, solutions from the web or prior offerings, or any assistance during an exam.

Regret policy: 24-hour self-report window via this form. Timely self-reports typically result in a zero on the assignment only. Non-reported violations face much more severe consequences.
